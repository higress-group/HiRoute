## S1 — Technical announcement and scope

OpenAI, September 8, 2026, with a September 10 update.
https://openai.com/index/navier-stokes-solution/

OpenAI announced an analytical argument and Lean formalization for a finite-time
singularity in three-dimensional Navier–Stokes flow with a smooth external force.
It describes initially smooth motion and finite energy. This concerns the forced
case, not a solution of the unforced Navier–Stokes regularity question. OpenAI
says it used an internal model and coordinated agents, then formal verification.
Its statement recognizes concurrent forced-Euler work by Levent Alpöge and
Tristan Buckmaster and denies that their unpublished work influenced its result.
These process and attribution statements are the company's account, not an
independent audit by the writer. A machine-checked formal argument and an
accessible explanation for students serve different purposes.

## S2 — A mathematician's response

Scott Aaronson, September 15, 2026, “The Age of Wonders and Terrors.”
https://scottaaronson.blog/?p=10062

Aaronson reports a sharp change in his expectations after the recent mathematical
advances. He worries about the future role of professional theorem proving and
about how to credit work when prompting, explaining and being accountable for
an argument come apart. His anxiety is a personal interpretation, not a measured
forecast that all mathematical jobs will disappear. He supports the Math and AI
declaration's concern for understanding and mathematical culture. The disagreement
is not simply whether a formal result is correct; it also concerns what counts
as a contribution people can understand and build on.

## S3 — Collective declaration

Math and AI declaration, published September 11, 2026.
https://mathandai.org/

The signatories distinguish solving famous problems from developing understanding.
They argue that discussions, careful exposition, teaching and connections to
earlier work turn results into shared mathematical knowledge. Rapid announcements
without clear writeups or credit can disrupt that process. Training on difficult
problems can develop skills even when the immediate answer is not the main goal.
The declaration acknowledges that AI could enhance mathematical study, while
calling for urgent choices by researchers and companies about how it is used.
This is a statement of values and risks, not evidence that every AI proof lacks
insight or that the community unanimously rejects AI.

## S4 — Institutional response

OpenAI, September 21, 2026, mathematics and AI advisory-group announcement.
https://openai.com/index/advisory-group-on-mathematics-and-ai/

OpenAI says its internal model has solved more than 100 longstanding problems.
The announcement is a company claim; the packet does not independently verify
those results. It announces an independent, unpaid advisory group to help with
mathematical significance, review, dissemination, academic standards and tools.
Members can comment publicly and choose additional members. The group does not
advise on the pace of internal mathematical progress. Its existence therefore
shows a response to concerns, but does not establish that all concerns are resolved
or that the group controls model development.

## S5 — Publication capacity

arXiv, October 1, 2026, updated submission-rate policy.
https://blog.arxiv.org/2026/10/01/updated-rate-limit-policy/

arXiv reports 9,869 submissions in September 2016, 20,569 in September 2024, and
40,363 in September 2026, plus almost 9,000 support tickets in the latest month.
It discusses both legitimate growth and low-quality or thin submissions, including
AI-related pressure; these figures do not isolate AI's causal share. The policy
allows two submissions per submitter per calendar month and three active
submissions. Categories share the limit; rejected submissions count. A coauthor
is not charged merely because someone else submits their joint paper. arXiv
allows disclosed AI use meeting its standards. This is not a blanket ban on
AI-written work or a two-paper cap on every coauthor.

## S6 — A different account of value

Matilde Marcolli, “Duet for the End of Math,” Caltech-hosted essay, 2026.
https://www.its.caltech.edu/~matilde/AImathNew.pdf

Marcolli questions why a small set of prestigious conjectures dominates rewards
and professional identity. She argues for valuing originality, connections across
areas, collaboration and mathematical creativity more broadly. She contrasts this
with a competitive view centered on a limited stock of famous problems. Her
position is that communities can change what they reward; automated problem
solving need not exhaust the reasons people create mathematics. This is a proposed
change in values, not evidence that such institutional reform has already happened.

## S7 — Reported human context

Jordana Cepelewicz, reporting for Quanta Magazine, October 5, 2026.
Provenance: Quanta Magazine, reporting on AI and the mathematics community.

Cepelewicz reports attending Ken Ono's September 10 Berkeley talk with roughly
150 students and researchers. A scheduled 50-minute event lasted more than two
hours amid anxious questions about careers and responsibility. Her reporting also
describes differing reactions: some researchers anticipate useful new work
interpreting and teaching AI results, while others worry that dependence on them
could weaken training and the appeal of a mathematical career. This is an
attributed reported scene, not an event you witnessed, and does not establish a
survey of mathematicians' views. The original feature is withheld from this packet.
